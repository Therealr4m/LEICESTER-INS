Loft Boarding in Leicester

Make room for storage while allowing for insulation and airflow.

A practical storage area

Loft boarding can provide a more usable surface for storage and access. You may only need a small area near the hatch, rather than boarding the whole loft. Start with what you want to store and how often you need access.

A boarded loft is not the same as a habitable room or a loft conversion. The structure, allowable loads and access must be suitable for the proposed use. Heavy items should not be stored without appropriate advice.

Plan boarding and insulation together

Boards laid directly on shallow joists can compress insulation or leave too little space for it. Raised boarding may be suitable in some lofts, allowing insulation underneath without squeezing it flat.

Ventilation routes need to remain open. Any changes around the hatch, cables and pipework should be considered as part of the same plan. Tell us if you intend to add insulation now or later.

Agree the area and access

The quote should identify which part of the loft will be boarded, the proposed system and any work to the hatch or access. Structural concerns should be resolved before installation.

For an initial enquiry, describe your existing hatch and ladder, what is currently stored and the approximate area you have in mind. Include your postcode so the location can be checked.

Common Questions

Does boarding turn a loft into a bedroom?

No. Creating a habitable room involves different structural, access, fire safety and building requirements. Storage boarding is a separate service.

Does the entire loft need boarding?

Not necessarily. A smaller area or access route may meet your needs. The layout should reflect the intended use and the structure.
